# Ralph and Dave's OSHA Nightmare: first playable

Greybox prototype: first-person stacking, jumping and forklift-riding. Three.js + Rapier + Vite.

The crew: **Dave** climbs, **Ralph** drives the forklift. Incident banners and CCTV replays name who did what to whom.

**Play:** https://eric-birdsall.github.io/osha-nightmare/ (every push to `main` redeploys via GitHub Actions).

```bash
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the printed URL in Chrome. Click the canvas to capture the mouse.

## The order

Every shelf in every bay is stocked with boxes (about 450), each with a unique 3-digit SKU on a small barcode label on all four sides. Every SKU has its own look: a box colour and a made-up brand logo ("Zappop", "Omnio", "Pixelex"...), the same on every box of that SKU, and about 15% of products are stamped **FRAGILE** (`objects.fragileChance`). The pick list is a clipboard hanging in the forklift cab (on the front-left guard post; Ralph's crutches lean beside his seat, and he has a cast on his left leg, which is why he never gets off the truck), not on the HUD: Ralph reads it from the seat, and Dave has to walk over and look in, or ask Ralph (in single player, swap to Ralph to check it). It lists two SKUs, a quantity of each, and their Rack-Bay-Shelf pick locations, picked at random each game from the upstairs racks:

- **Line 1:** 3 boxes from shelf 2 of any upstairs rack (reach it from the forks or a ladder).
- **Line 2:** 1 or 2 boxes from shelf 3, the top, of a different upstairs rack (reach it from the forks near full height, or from a ladder Ralph lifts on the forks).

**Backstock.** The mezzanine is backstock. Line 1's pick face is always one box short: there's a gap where the last box should be, and an orange **BACKSTOCK → H-4-1**-style sign hangs under that shelf's beam. The rest of that SKU (a full row of three) sits at that location on a mezzanine rack, on the bottom shelf, in reach from the deck. A 2-box line 2 is sometimes short too (`order.backstockChance`). Other stock has backstock as well: about 6 more pick faces carry BACKSTOCK signs (`order.extraBackstock`), each pointing to a real row of that face's SKU on the mezzanine, so a sign on its own doesn't mean it's your box; check the SKU against the clipboard. The clipboard keeps a tally ("2/3") as boxes go onto a pallet.

The order boxes look like every other box, and the right location usually also holds a decoy with a near-miss SKU (two digits swapped, or one digit off) **printed in the same colour and brand**, so check the number against the clipboard. Aim at a box to see its SKU in the pick-up prompt. A line only ticks off when the full quantity of the matching SKU is on one pallet. Get everything onto one pallet, set it down on the floor in the yellow **WRAP** square, back the forklift out of the square, and leave the pallet still until it's wrapped, then drive the wrapped pallet into the **truck at this order's dock** and set it down there. A wrapped pallet that's short of anything is turned away at the dock ("missing 1 × SKU 4881"). To fix it, put the missing boxes on top of the wrapped load and bring it back to the wrap square: it gets wrapped again with the new box included.

The warehouse is 38 × 46.4 m and 12 m to the roof: corrugated steel on joists, rows of fluorescent strip lights on chains, exposed silver ductwork with diffuser drops, and four giant yellow ceiling fans. A big yellow N, S, E or W hangs on each wall. The plan is in `src/world/layout.js`. **The north-west corner is the basement**, 1 m down. Dave gets down by a flight of six steps in the west lane, from the basement's south edge beside Rack A's west end (the opening in the railing is there), going down northward into the basement's south-west corner, south of Dock 1. The forklift has a ramp (12%) along the north wall that runs down westward into it. Yellow railings with a hazard-painted lip run round the drop. The basement holds the painted **FORKLIFT PARKING** bay with its wall charger, in the very corner against the west wall with the truck facing east: it's Ralph's start spot (each round starts with a drive along the north wall and up the ramp) and where the night shift drops a tipped forklift. **The two loading docks are in the basement too**: Dock 1 on the west wall, Dock 2 on the north wall. Each order ships from one picked at random (the clipboard says which): its roll-up door is up, its lamp is green, and a trailer is backed up with its floor level with the basement floor, so Ralph drives the wrapped load straight in and leaves it there to finish the order. The other dock is shut with a CLOSED sign. The forklift's mast telescopes: collapsed (forks under 1 m) it fits through the door and inside the trailer; above that the inner mast rises and won't. Seven racks (A–G, six bays each, single-deep) stand in one block on the main floor. **Rack A** has its back against the basement's railing; south of it come three back-to-back pairs, B/C, D/E and F/G, every row 4.4 m from the next (tight pick aisles A/B, C/D and E/F). The block's east end caps line up with the basement's east edge. A narrow **west lane** (about 3.8 m) runs down the outside of the block and a wide **east lane** past its east end. On the main floor right against the basement's east edge, between the ramp and the spot above the basement's LADDERS stand, is the **rotary-arm stretch wrapper** over the WRAP square: four yellow posts and a steel gantry (3.9 m up) with a motor in the middle, signs reading STRETCH WRAPPER and KEEP CLEAR WHILE WRAPPING, and a control panel with a red stop button. While a load wraps, the arm swings the film carriage round it, riding up and down the load. The posts and the gantry beams are solid, so forks raised too high in there hit the beams. A wrapped load goes out the wrapper's east side, a few metres north onto the top of the ramp, and down it into the basement and the docks. Its west posts stand a metre from the missing railing. Down the east side of the building, 9.2 m in from the east wall, are six **packing stations** in two staggered rows running north–south (PACK 1–6), with the **packing line** between them: a second conveyor, 1.6 m wide, that carries boxes north at 0.8 m/s from the south end of the island to the wrapper and drops them onto a pallet waiting just south of the wrapper, with about a metre clear between the pallet and the wrapper's frame. The back panels make a backboard. The two rows are staggered by half a bench, with 1.4 m gaps, so each gap in one row looks straight across the belt at a panel in the other: from the gap, or from the floor outside lined up with it, Dave punts a box at that panel and it drops onto the belt. The stations are workbenches with a tall solid back panel (2.8 m) facing the belt, and bubble wrap and kraft paper rolls overhead, a monitor, a scale, a half-packed box, flat cardboard underneath, an anti-fatigue mat and a bin, plus a stack of empty pallets at the island's south end. They have no job yet, but they're physics props: each bench (with everything on it) is one body of about 90 kg with grippy feet and its weight up high. A forklift hit faster than walking pace also gives a bench a push near the top (like the racks' wobble), so a full-speed hit knocks one over while a slow nudge just shoves it; side-on hits mostly shove them along. The bins and the pallet stack are loose props too, and blasts fling the lot. Along the east wall runs a **conveyor**, waist high (0.8 m) and 22 m long, from just north of the electrical panel to the north-east corner: its belt carries anything resting on it north at 1.2 m/s and drops it off the far end onto a waiting pallet, with a striped backstop beyond it to catch anything that flies. Yellow guide rails over the last 2 m of each belt funnel what's on it to the middle, so boxes land on the pallet; once the pallet's piled high, boxes queue at the end of the belt. Boxes, pallets, barrels, ladders, and Dave, who rides it if he climbs on (getting off the end is a short, harmless drop). **The mezzanine** runs along the south wall from the west wall: a steel deck 3 m up and 5.6 m deep holding the backstock racks H and I (only two shelves high: 1.5 m and 3.0 m above the deck), H along the front railing and I against the south wall, facing each other across a 2.6 m aisle: room to walk down and to set up a ladder in front of either rack (from its top step Dave reaches the top shelf). Dave climbs the stairs at its east end to a landing at deck height; there's also a **pallet drop**, a gap in its front railing past the racks' east end, where the forklift (forks reach 3.5 m) can set a pallet, or Dave on one, onto the deck. Under it is the **break room**: MEN and WOMEN toilets, an out-of-order water fountain, a snack machine and a drinks machine (heavy, top-heavy physics props), a table and two chairs, and a counter with a microwave and a coffee maker. The forklift fits under the deck with its mast down. Locations read Rack-Bay-Shelf. Bays are numbered 1–6 from the west end of each rack, and shelves 1–3 from the bottom of that rack (1.5 m, 3.0 m, 4.5 m above its floor; the mezzanine's racks stop at shelf 2). Each rack has a rack sign on both end caps, and its aisle face has a location tag on each shelf beam.

## Controls

| | Gamepad | Keyboard / mouse |
|---|---|---|
| **Climber** move / look | Left / right stick | WASD / mouse or IJKL |
| Punt the box in hand (top box of a stack): hold to aim, let go to kick it | RB | R |
| Stand a fallen ladder back up (hands empty, near it) | Hold RB 1 s | Hold R 1 s |
| Flip a tipped-over pallet back flat (hands empty, near it) | Hold X 1 s | Hold E 1 s |
| Jump | A | Space |
| Pick up / put down a box, pick up / set up / fold a ladder, hold on to the forks | X | E |
| **Driver** steer / look | Left / right stick | A, D / mouse or IJKL |
| Accelerate / brake (then reverse) | Left stick up / down (or RT / LT) | W / S |
| Forks up / down | RB / LB | R / F |
| Drift (hold while turning) | B | Space |
| Back to the start (Dave to his first spot, the forklift upright in its bay) | Hold Y 1 s | Hold Q 1 s |
| Swap character (single player) | View | Tab |
| Pause | Menu | Esc |

New players: **How to Play** on the title and pause menus is a one-page guide (the job, who's who, the order step by step, reaching shelves, moving boxes, incidents, hazards, resets and tips). On a pad or keys, up and down scroll it.

Co-op uses split screen, with the Climber on top. Gamepad 1 plays the Climber and gamepad 2 the Driver. If only one pad is connected, player 2 uses keyboard and mouse. Pause → Swap Roles trades characters. **Invert Look** (on the Controls screen, from the title or pause menu) flips up/down for the mouse, sticks and I/K; it's remembered in this browser. The pause menu also shows a floor plan of the warehouse with Dave and Ralph marked on it.

## Layout

- `src/config.js`: every tuning value (speeds, masses, lift rate, friction, ragdoll thresholds)
- `src/main.js`: renderer, fixed 1/60 s loop, menus, pointer lock
- `src/ui/osha-ui.css` (+ `assets/heavy-distress-mask.svg`): the OSHA Nightmare UI kit, distressed 3D block lettering (`osha-text`, `--yellow` / `--ivory` / `--red`) and a metal sign plate (`osha-plate`), used for the PAUSED, Order Complete and Controls headings and the in-game INCIDENT banner. Its font, Black Ops One, loads with the other Google Fonts in `index.html`. `src/logo-menu.png` is the title screen's logo, a smaller copy of `src/logo.png`
- `src/world/layout.js`: **the warehouse layout as data** (west-side things are placed from the west wall, dock-side things from the east wall, and the wrapper and packing stations from the racks' east end, so changing `room.halfX` narrows or widens the dock side): room size, lower level, ramp and stairs, racking (bays, bay width, shelf heights) and every rack row, docks, the WRAP square, the packing stations, the parking bay, the hazards, LADDERS stands, loose boxes, pallets, spawn points, ceiling fans and ducts, and the CCTV cameras. Everything below builds from it, so a new layout is mostly a matter of editing these numbers. Racks are `rack(name, z0, face, base)` or `pair(north, south, z0, base)` (back to back); each can have its own `x0` and `bays`.
- `src/world/level.js`: builds the warehouse, racks, stock, zones, initial boxes and pallets from the layout
- `src/world/parking.js`, `wrapper.js`, `packing.js`, `conveyor.js`, `mezzanine.js`, `props.js`, `spills.js`, `floorplan.js`: the forklift parking bay, the stretch wrapper, the packing stations, the conveyor, the mezzanine and break room, loose physics props, the hidden spills (their no-go zones come from the layout), and the pause screen's floor plan (drawn from the layout at startup)
- `src/entities/`: `climber.js` (character controller, carry, ragdoll triggers), `forklift.js`, `box.js`, `pallet.js`
- `src/systems/`: `input.js`, `ragdoll.js`, `wrapping.js`, `game.js` (order state, swap, win), `hud.js`, `audio.js`

## Implementation notes (where it differs from or adds to the spec)

- **Forks** are a separate dynamic carriage body on a motorized prismatic joint up the mast. The solver therefore knows the fork velocity: loads lift smoothly, their weight presses on the truck, and racks block the forks.
- **The Climber's capsule is query-only.** It never pushes or blocks bodies in the solver. Walking into a box or pallet shoves it; walking into the forklift or a rack doesn't move it.
- **Forklift hits:** if the part of the forklift touching the Climber is moving faster than `climber.hitSpeed` (1.5 m/s), it's an incident: head-on, reversing, sideswipes, tail swing, or the forks scooping them up. Slower contact is a nudge, and walking into a parked or crawling truck is never an incident.
- **Racks are dynamic and wobbly.** Each rack is one light rigid body (60 kg per bay) with its shelves, labels and anything on them, and grippy feet so it rocks rather than skates. A raw forklift hit mostly pushes on the uprights' feet, so the game adds a "wobble": when the forklift, a falling ladder or a falling neighbouring rack newly hits a standing rack, the impact also rocks the top of that rack in the direction of travel, once per hit and capped so nothing gets launched. Back-to-back racks are tied together, so a hit rocks both racks of the pair. Forklift hits at 1.5 m/s just bump a rack; ~1.8 m/s usually knocks it over and 2 m/s+ always does. A ladder toppling against a rack knocks it over too. Aisles are narrower than the racks are tall (5.2 m), so falling racks take out their neighbours like dominoes, and Rack A can topple backwards over the railing into the basement. Knocked-over racks are incidents, blamed on Ralph or the ladder; a falling rack hitting Dave is one too. Shelf stock counts toward a rack's weight when it's hit. **Resetting the racking:** near a rack that's been knocked over, shoved off its spot, or has lost stock off its shelves, hold E/X for 3 s. Every disturbed rack stands back up where it belongs and the stock that fell goes back on its shelves, so an order box can't end up out of reach for good. Boxes Dave has picked up and moved stay where he put them, and anything on a pallet or wrapped load stays put. A yellow ring fills round the crosshair while you hold. If the forklift is parked (or lying) where a rack goes, it's sent back to its starting bay; Dave, loose pallets, ladders and stray boxes in the way are moved out into the aisle. Tuning is under `racks` in `config.js`.
- **Rising platforms carry the Climber up directly.** A character controller can't push out of a surface it's already inside. Being lifted into a beam overhead counts as an incident ("crushed").
- **Reach** is 1.5 m from chest height along the aim ray, so boxes at your feet can be grabbed.
- **Height math.** The pallet deck rests on the forks, so at max lift the Climber's feet are at about 3.55 m, level with the top shelf (4.5 m) within reach. From the top step of an A-frame ladder (1.55 m) Dave reaches shelf 2 but not shelf 3.
- **Mantle.** In the air and pushing into a ledge within 0.35 m above your feet, you climb onto it. This is what makes the jump from the forks to the 4.5 m beam possible.
- **Speed and drifting.** The forklift tops out at 6 m/s (13 mph on the dash). A normal grip turn can only corner so hard (`drift.gripLatAccel`), so at speed it runs wide, and full lock at top speed leans it right to the edge of tipping. **Hold Space / B while turning** and the truck does a little hop (`drift.hop`, like Mario Kart) as the rear tyres let go: the tail swings round at a steady rate whatever the speed, and the truck slides at an angle (about 15-25°) while keeping most of its speed, leaving skid marks and tyre smoke. A drift can't tip the truck (the sideways force goes through the centre of mass while sliding, and grip comes back gradually). Hold a good drift for 0.8 s and orange sparks turn blue: let go for a **boost** up to about 8 m/s for 0.8 s, after which it settles back to top speed. Ralph can't see the sparks from the seat, so a drift meter above his dashboard fills orange while drifting, turns blue with BOOST READY once the boost is earned, and shows BOOST! when it fires; the cab view widens by `drift.boostFov` degrees during the boost and eases back over about a second. Pallets on the forks ride on friction, so a hard drift can fling them. Tuning is under `forklift.drift` in `config.js`.
- **Tipping:** the tires' sideways grip acts at the floor, so hard cornering rolls the truck. Roll damping (`forklift.rollDamping`) soaks up the lurch of a quick flick of the wheel, and grip turns are capped (above), so a normal turn rarely tips it; boosting into a grip turn can. Raised forks make it tippier. **A tipped forklift explodes**, with the same huge blast as a FRAGILE box (30 m reach): everything loose is flung, racks go over, and Dave is thrown if he's in range ("Dave caught the forklift blast", on Ralph). Ralph is fine, somehow. After 4 s the night shift puts the truck back in its parking bay.
- **Ramp driving:** the forklift's drive holds it against the slope (like a hydrostatic drive), so on the ramp it drives, brakes and coasts as on the flat: it doesn't run away downhill, and it can stop and reverse back up.
- **Dashboard:** a screen built into a sloped yellow binnacle on the dash, centred behind the steering wheel, shows a speedometer (mph, needle plus digital readout, red above 8 mph), D/R gear lights, and a fork-height bar with the reading in metres and ticks at the 1.5 m and 3.0 m shelf heights. These replaced the driver's on-screen gear and fork-height boxes.
- **Stacks and punting:** picking up a box also lifts whatever is stacked on it, up to 3 boxes (`climber.maxCarry`); aim at the bottom box to take the stack. A taller stack is too much, so Dave takes the top box. He walks a little slower with 2 or 3 (`climber.stackSpeed`), and putting down sets the whole stack down as a neat column where the ghost shows. **Punting:** R / RB punts the box in hand (the top one of a stack): hold it to aim, and a dotted arc and a ghost box (red for FRAGILE) show exactly where it will land; let go and Dave drops the box onto his foot and boots it along the crosshair. It always leaves his boot at the same speed, 10 m/s plus a small lob (`climber.puntSpeed`, `puntLift`), so aim higher to punt further. A punted box flies without spinning and lands dead: landing on top of something, it stops right there; hitting a wall or a panel side-on, it drops straight down onto whatever's below. That's what the ghost shows, so it's always where the box ends up (until something else moves it, like a conveyor). Handy for punting boxes onto a pallet, the packing line, or up onto raised forks. Punting is never an incident.
- **Flipping pallets:** a pallet that's ended up on its side, on its end or upside down can be put right: with empty hands, within about 1.8 m of it, hold E / X for a second (the ring round the crosshair fills) and it drops back flat where it lies, pointing the way it was. A quick tap of E / X still picks up a box as usual. If something's in the way of it lying flat, it says so. Tuning is `climber.palletFlipRange` and `palletFlipHoldTime`.
- **Spills.** Each order has 2-3 spills (2.4-3.2 m across) at random spots on flat floor, upstairs or down. There's no warning sign, and they're only a slight wet-looking tint and faint sheen on the concrete (water, oil, soda or goo, all close to the floor colour), so they're easy to run over by accident. They're not on the floor plan either. On a spill Dave has almost no grip, so he slides on with whatever speed he came in with and can barely steer or stop, but never falls. The forklift's wet wheels lose most of their traction (`spill.forkliftGrip`), so it barely brakes or turns and slides straight on, maybe into a rack. Boxes, pallets and ladders slide across spills too. Tuning is under `spill` in `config.js`.
- **More OSHA violations** (`src/world/hazards.js`, placed under `hazards` in `layout.js`):
  - **The sparking outlet:** on the north wall behind the wrapper, a scorched outlet with cube taps stacked in it, a DO NOT UNPLUG!! sign, and power strips daisy-chained across the floor. Every few seconds it shoots a burst of cartoon sparks, with a flash and a crackle.
  - **The electrical waterfall:** on the east wall just past the conveyor's south end, a leaking pipe joint (duct-taped) drips onto a HIGH VOLTAGE · KEEP DRY panel. Water streaks down its front into a puddle that's slippery like a spill, beside a bucket catching none of it. Now and then a drip shorts it out: sparks, a blue flash and a zap.
  - **The missing railing:** a 2.5 m section of the basement's railing (on its east edge, right by the ramp) is gone, replaced by sagging hazard tape and a tiny WATCH YOUR STEP sign. The tape stops nothing: Dave walks straight through and drops into the basement, and the forklift can drive off the edge.
  - **Mystery barrels:** five unlabelled drums (some sprayed "???") by the south wall just east of the mezzanine stairs, each leaking a brightly coloured liquid into a slippery puddle. They're physics props: the forklift or a blast knocks them over, and a barrel that goes over gushes a bigger puddle where it lands.
  - **Blocked fire exits:** two lit EXIT doors (one on the west wall by the west lane, one in the south-east corner) with FIRE EXIT · KEEP CLEAR signs, buried behind solid piles of pallets, boxes and plywood.
  - Each one has a name in incident reports ("The leaking electrical panel", "The missing railing"...), random spills stay off them, and the fire exits and barrels are on the floor plan.
- **FRAGILE boxes explode.** Punt a FRAGILE box, let go of one with nowhere to set it (the orange ghost), or get knocked flat while carrying one, and it goes off on the first thing it hits, like a grenade: a huge fireball fills the warehouse and everything loose within 30 m (most of the building: boxes, pallets, ladders, whole racks, and Dave) is flung outward and up, and the forklift gets shoved. The roof keeps the debris indoors. It's an incident on Dave ("It said FRAGILE, Dave"), and racks it knocks over are incidents of their own. The box itself is restocked on its shelf, so an order box is never lost. Setting a FRAGILE box down carefully (green ghost) is safe. Tuning is under `blast` in `config.js`.
- **Holding on to the forks:** anywhere on or right by the forks (on the blades, out at their tips, on a pallet or boxes sitting on them, or on the floor in front of lowered forks), E/X grabs the carriage. It works in mid-air too, with a bigger reach: jump or fall off a shelf at the forks, press E/X as you come past them, and Dave catches on and eases down onto whatever's under him on the forks, safe however far he fell. Dave then rides it rigidly and can't be thrown off however fast Ralph drives, drifts, swerves or crashes; in exchange he can't walk or look round. He stands with his back to the truck and his arms behind him gripping the backrest (from the cab you see his back), facing out along the forks with his eyes level, until he lets go (E/X again). He can still get hurt by something landing on him (a falling box, a toppling rack) or by the forks slamming into the floor.
- **Back to the start:** holding Q / Y for a full second sends the character you're playing straight back to where they started the round. Dave drops whatever he's carrying and gets out of a ragdoll, a wrap or a ladder; the forklift goes back to its parking bay, upright and at rest with its forks down. A ring fills while you hold it (a quick tap does nothing), and you have to let go before it'll do it again.
- **Forks can drop fast.** They rise at 0.4 m/s, but holding F/LB speeds the descent up to 3 m/s (2 m/s²); let go and they stop hard, but held all the way down they hit the ground at whatever speed they've reached. **If they hit the ground at near full speed (over 2.7 m/s, `climber.legBreakSpeed`) with Dave riding them** (on the blades, holding on, on a load on the forks, or on a ladder Ralph is carrying) **his legs break** at the moment of impact: Ralph's incident ("Ralph broke Dave's legs"). Only a drop of about 1.8 m or more held all the way down gets that fast; let go before the bottom, or tap, and Dave is fine. Loads ride the slam out.
- **CCTV incident replays:** eight security cameras are mounted high on the walls. 1.5 s after every incident, the game freezes and replays the moment at half speed in black and white. It cuts between up to three cameras with a clear view of the Climber, and any button skips. The replay is built from a rolling 5 s recording of everything that moves (`src/systems/replay.js`, tuning under `replay` in `config.js`).
- **Ralph's incidents:** tipping the forklift is an incident ("Ralph tipped the forklift"), with a replay that follows the truck. A rack falling over is an incident too, blamed on Ralph if the forklift touched it in the last few seconds. Racks falling within 6 s of each other count as one domino incident with a growing banner ("Ralph took out Racks D and E").
- **Incident reports:** after each CCTV replay, a filled-in Form 300 appears: case number, employee and job title, shift time, location (rack and bay, aisle, or zone), what happened, ticked root causes (Dave, Ralph, Gravity, the racking, the ladder, the wrapper), corrective action, days away from work, the cameras that witnessed it, and Ralph's signature. Any button files it and play resumes. The Order Complete screen now counts incidents rather than falls.
- **A-frame ladders:** four folded step ladders hang on two yellow **LADDERS** stands: one in the basement against its east edge (where the stairs used to come down, below the wrapper), and one on the top landing of the mezzanine stairs, against the south wall. There's none on the main floor: fetch one up the stairs, or have Ralph lift one up on the forks. Tap E/X at a stand (or at a ladder lying on the floor) to shoulder one: Dave's hands are full and he walks at 70%. A ghost A-frame shows where it will go, steps facing Dave: green on flat, clear floor at his level, red on the stairs, the ramp, a shelf, a pallet, against a railing or a rack. Tap E/X on green to set it up. Walk into the steps to climb on; W/S (or the stick) climbs up and down, and forward climbs the way you're looking (look down at the floor and W takes you down), and stepping down off the bottom gets off. From the top step (1.55 m) Dave reaches shelf 2 but not shelf 3, and he can pick and place boxes, and climb with one in his hands. **Dave can jump off a ladder**, a normal jump the way he's looking (onto a pallet, the forks, another level), and a jump off the top step down to the floor doesn't hurt him; but pushing off always kicks the ladder over, so there's no landing back on it. The forklift can knock a set-up ladder over, or shove it faster than 1.5 m/s, and with Dave on it that's Ralph's incident. Ralph can also slide the forks in from behind (between the legs, under the spreader tray) and lift the ladder, Dave and all, which puts shelf 3 in reach. Tap E/X next to a set-up ladder to fold it back onto your shoulder. **A ladder that's been knocked over** (or dropped folded on the floor) can be stood straight back up where it lies: with empty hands, within about 1.8 m of it, hold R / RB for a second (the ring round the crosshair fills), and it springs up as an A-frame with its steps facing Dave, if there's flat, clear floor there. Ladders stay wherever they're left. Tuning is under `ladder` in `config.js`.
- **Wrap countdown:** a pallet in the WRAP square gets a ring on the floor around it. The outline is red while the pallet is off the floor (still on the forks), tipped, or while any part of the forklift is in the square, with a hint saying which, white while it's moving, and it fills yellow over the second it must sit still before wrapping starts. The first pallet into the square also shows a one-time hint.
- **Placement ghost:** while carrying a box, a translucent copy shows exactly where it will land if you put it down now (same calculation as the put-down itself), including on a raised pallet. Climber's camera only.
- **See-through cab:** the overhead guard is a grid of bars, and the carriage is an open frame, so the driver can see up through the roof and the load through the backrest.
- **Throw threshold:** the support must be moving at 1.5 m/s or more and accelerate by more than 10 m/s² (smoothed). A full brake (about 5.3 m/s²) does not throw you; clipping a rack at speed does.

## Playtest console

`window.osha` is exposed in the dev build:

```js
osha.CONFIG.forklift.topSpeed = 5   // live-tune (most values are read every step)
osha.scenario(3.5, -6, -12.2, Math.PI / 2)  // forklift in aisle A/B, forks up, pallet + climber on them
osha.sim(2, {}, { throttle: 1 })     // step physics headlessly with scripted actions
osha.override = { driver: { liftUp: 1 } }  // inject actions into live play
```
